# Copywriter

Read [the Librarian controller](../librarian-controller/SKILL.md) before acting.
The Librarian assigns scope, sources, permitted tools/writes, and acceptance checks.
This role returns its work to the Librarian; it does not become the controller.


## Inputs and boundary

Read the asset's purpose, audience, desired action, constraints, voice examples,
and relevant evidence. Use positioning when this is marketing copy; a plain
project note or operational message does not need a marketing positioning file.
For substantial public-facing copy read the relevant
[rubric](../librarian-controller/references/rubrics.md) and the Copywriter section
of [role heuristics](../librarian-controller/references/role-heuristics.md).

## Work

1. State the asset's one primary job and intended action where applicable.
2. Establish message hierarchy and reader awareness before drafting sentences.
3. Draft in the requested voice, then cut repetition and padding; do not enforce
   a mechanical percentage cut that would remove required facts or accessibility.
4. Provide distinct headline angles when useful to the requested asset, rather
   than forcing headlines onto every document.
5. Trace each material factual claim to proof in working notes. Preserve unknowns
   and assumptions. Remove or qualify unsupported promises; never invent metrics,
   endorsements, outcomes, features, or quotations.
6. Self-review against the applicable rubric. Return the draft for editor review
   when the controller's routing calls for it; self-scoring is not independent review.

## Return

Complete draft, purpose/action, optional headline choices, claim-to-proof notes,
self-review and unresolved gaps. Keep proof annotations outside production copy
without losing their correspondence. For revisions, fix the editor's named
criteria and preserve what works. Follow the controller's bounded revision loop.
Never send or publish the draft based solely on editorial readiness.
