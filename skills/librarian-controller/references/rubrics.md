# Editorial rubrics

Shared scoring guides for writing and editorial review. Score against the user's
goal, not generic engagement. Truth and evidence-status checks come first; an
unsupported factual claim is BLOCKED regardless of score. Explain each criterion's
score and the correction needed. Weights total ten; criteria are task-specific.

| Asset | Threshold | Criteria (maximum points) |
|---|---|---|
| Short post | 7/10 | Specific opening (3); one developed idea (2); evidence/detail (2); serves goal (2); effective ending (1) |
| Article/newsletter | 7/10 | Distinct reason to exist (2); navigable structure (2); evidence throughout (2); appropriate voice (2); ending (1); honest length (1) |
| Landing/sales copy | 8/10 | Clear offer within the opening screen (2); clear primary action (2); claims proved (2); likely objection addressed (2); concrete specificity (2) |
| Sequence email | 7/10 | Distinct job in sequence (3); honest subject (2); primary action (2); appropriate brevity (2); voice (1) |

Specific opening: grounded in this author/product and audience, not a generic
curiosity gap. Evidence may be a verified example or relevant first-hand detail;
do not invent a metric to earn points. Goal alignment means the requested outcome,
not reach by default. A conclusion can fulfill the requested format; do not ban
summaries where they help the reader. A justified multi-action interface is not
automatically a failure: judge its primary path against the actual brief.

For other formats, editor states observable acceptance criteria suited to the
assignment before evaluating; numerical scoring is optional. The controller owns
the revision limit and final integration. READY indicates editorial quality only.

## Claim and verdict example

An input says “doubles revenue” `[assumed: no supporting test]`. Rephrasing it as
“may double revenue” still lacks evidence for the quantitative promise. Remove
that promise from the draft or replace it with a genuinely supported, narrower
claim and its source; carry the omitted claim and missing proof in working notes.
An older draft's READY verdict does not transfer to changed text. Editor reviews
the actual new draft/version and its claim notes before returning a new verdict.
